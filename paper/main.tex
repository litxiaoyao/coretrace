\begin{abstract}
Language models can answer two constituent questions from the same evidence yet fail to compose their answers. Across three models, composition fails on roughly half of the sources with correct constituent answers. A training-fitted readout nevertheless recovers the bridge paragraph in 72.2--86.0\% of these failures. To understand this discrepancy, we factorize attention into paragraph access mass and the conditional reading pattern within the paragraph. Crossed interventions reveal that calibrating access increases the utility of direct-query reading patterns, with interaction estimates of 5--16 percentage points. Guided by this diagnosis, we propose \method, which uses attention-derived address information to select evidence and adapts the selected heads with fixed projection restoration. With fewer than 1\% trainable model parameters, \method\ raises composed accuracy from \scores{26.9}{29.2}{36.4}\% to \scores{66.8}{67.2}{69.7}\% on Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, and Llama-3.1-8B. Ambiguous-candidate comparisons connect the recovered address to better answers, and reciprocal fact exchanges show that repaired predictions follow changed local values in 76--80\% of cases. These results establish a functional interaction between evidence access and local reading and demonstrate its utility for improving contextual composition.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Impact Statement}
This work studies how language models use supplied evidence and develops targeted interventions for more reliable contextual question answering. Its findings can inform systems that connect facts across documents. We evaluate reasoning and general capabilities to characterize the effects of adaptation. Experiments use public question-answering datasets. Downstream applications should assess evidence quality, answer accuracy, and task-specific reliability under their deployment conditions.
