\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Contextual composition can fail even when both constituent answers are correct and the bridge address remains recoverable. By factorizing attention, we show that paragraph access changes the usefulness of relation-conditioned reading. Reciprocal fact exchanges connect this interaction to predictions that follow local evidence. \method\ converts these findings into an input-driven repair: selected-head adaptation and attention-based evidence selection raise composed accuracy by 33.3--39.9 points with fewer than 1\% trainable model parameters. Resolving ambiguous candidates links the internal address signal directly to improved answers, while fixed projection restoration recovers capabilities affected by adaptation. Together, these results identify evidence access and local reading as complementary targets for understanding and improving contextual reasoning.
