\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Correct constituent answers do not guarantee successful composition under the same evidence. By localizing evidence-access heads and factorizing their attention, we connect this gap to the interaction between paragraph access and local reading. Bridge addresses remain recoverable in many failed compositions, yet the usefulness of a direct-query reading pattern changes with the attention allocated to its paragraph. Crossed interventions make this dependence measurable. \method\ translates the localized heads and address signals into an effective repair, improving composed accuracy by 33.3--39.9 percentage points with fewer than 1\% trainable model parameters. Its responses to reciprocal fact exchanges further demonstrate the use of local evidence. These findings establish access-dependent reading as a functional account of contextual composition and provide a concrete basis for improving it.
